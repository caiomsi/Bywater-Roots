About us

A garden for the whole neighborhood

Bywater Roots is a student concept for a community garden at Bywater Park in Cottonwood Heights, Utah. Here is the idea, the place and the plan.

Our mission

Our mission

To give every neighbor a place to grow food, meet the people next door and look after the pollinators we share the park with.

The story

Why this park, why this neighborhood

Bywater Park is a neighborhood park surrounded by homes at 3149 E Banbury Rd in Cottonwood Heights, managed by Cottonwood Heights Parks & Recreation. It already has a playground, baseball fields, a tennis court, a pavilion, picnic tables, restrooms and parking, and it sits near the Cottonwood Heights Recreation Center.

What it doesn't have is a place to grow things together. We propose turning an underused grassy edge of the park into a garden that families with kids, retirees, neighbors without yard space, local schools and scouts can all share.

Plots would run April through October, with winter workshops at the Recreation Center, so there is something to look forward to all year.

What we'd build

Six features, one shared garden

  • Raised beds

    Sturdy, tidy beds in two sizes with good soil, water and shared tools included.

  • ADA-height accessible beds

    Raised to a comfortable working height, placed beside the path, with priority for gardeners who need them.

  • Kids' learning plot

    A plot beside the playground where kids plant, tend and taste.

  • Pollinator & native-plant strip

    A flowering strip along the park edge that feeds bees and butterflies.

  • Compost station

    Turn garden scraps into next season's soil.

  • Shaded gathering area

    A spot by the pavilion to rest, share lunch and swap tips.

Our values

What we stand for

Inclusive

Beds for every age and ability, and a pay-what-you-can scholarship so cost is never the reason to stay home.

Sustainable

Compost, native plants and shared water and tools: growing food that takes care of the ground it grows in.

Neighbor-run

Built and tended by the people who live around the park, with the city's park team as a good neighbor.
